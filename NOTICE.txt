WHAT THE APP READS
- The route, sections, suggested levels, one-line descriptions, journal-activation blocks, fact prerequisites and cutoffs
  are generated from the game's own journal files, level tables and phase graphs by the scripts in tools/. They are shipped
  as data/route.json, data/derived.json, data/quest_graph.json and the reviewed inputs beside them.
- At run time the app reads your own copy of the game to build its quest-name index. It reads your save files read-only.
  It never writes to your saves folder or the game folder.
- The older wiki-era files data/audit_*.json, data/guide_*.json, data/guide_alias.json, data/legacy_route.json,
  data/legacy_events.json and data/legacy_feed.json are kept in the source checkout for comparison and are never
  loaded by the app. They are not part of the release package.

SOURCES AND ATTRIBUTION
- The legacy research contains information from the contributors of The Witcher Wiki (https://witcher.fandom.com),
  available under CC BY-SA 3.0. Changes were made: the text was summarised and reorganised into structured data.
- Facts in that legacy research were cross-checked against public walkthroughs (Gamer Guides, Fextralife, ConsolePulse,
  TheGamer, GameBanshee). Those pages are not redistributed and are no longer linked by the app.
- The 0.2 route and its rules do not depend on that research. Where a claim is generated, the app names the game file,
  fact or phase block behind it in the detail pane.

USE OF AI

  the development corpus, but the in-game quest log has the final word.

PRIVACY AND SAFETY
- The tool only reads your save files. It never writes to your saves folder or the game folder.
- It writes only its own settings (ticks, level, tags) and a cached quest index to %APPDATA%\Witcher3MissablesTracker.
- The tool's own code makes no network connections and sends no data (it contains no HTTP client). Links in the tool open
  in your browser only when you click them. The window is drawn by Microsoft Edge WebView2, a separate Windows component
  that is not part of this tool.
- The full source code is public so the program can be inspected and built independently.
